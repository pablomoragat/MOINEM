DASHBOARDS & ANALYTICS

Are traditional dashboards still enough?

Dashboards remain effective for monitoring. AI analytics adds another capability: investigating questions that arise after a metric is observed.

MOINEM Insights · September 27, 2026

A dashboard is excellent at answering known questions: how much was sold, how margin is evolving, which market is below budget or which KPI is out of range. Its strength is consistent monitoring.

The limit appears when a new question arises

After seeing a variance, users want to understand what explains it, whether it is concentrated in customers or products, whether price, volume or cost is responsible, and when the effect occurred.

What conversational analytics adds

A natural-language interface can let users ask ad-hoc questions without knowing SQL or the exact table structure. That flexibility should still rely on controlled metrics and business rules.

It is not dashboard versus AI. Dashboards can monitor while an analytical layer investigates, explains and explores.

Dashboard: continuous monitoring

  • Recurring KPIs.
  • Standard comparisons.
  • Visual alerts.
  • Executive and operational tracking.

AI analytics: exploration and explanation

  • New questions over existing data.
  • Dynamic rankings and segmentation.
  • Contribution analysis for variances.
  • Comparisons not anticipated in the original dashboard.
  • Executive summaries backed by calculations.

How to choose what to automate

If a question repeats every day and follows a stable structure, it likely belongs in a dashboard or alert. If it appears because of a new finding and requires exploration across dimensions, a flexible analytical layer may be more efficient.

A practical example

A dashboard shows margin below budget in Chile. The next questions are: which products explain the gap, is the effect driven by price or cost, and which customers concentrate the impact? This chain is difficult to anticipate completely in a static dashboard.
